## 文案

The star shapes you know first? Many aren’t constellations. 🌌
The sky is split into 88 official constellations. The Big Dipper isn’t one of them: it’s just the Great Bear’s rear and tail — a famous but unofficial pattern, an asterism. In China, though, Beidou (the Northern Dipper) is a star group of its own, marking the seasons and directions. It depends whose rules you use.
The Summer Triangle: Vega, Altair and Deneb, in three different constellations. Vega is 25 light-years away, Altair 17, Deneb over 1,000. And all three English names come from Arabic: Vega, “the swooping eagle”; Altair, “the flying eagle”; Deneb, “the hen’s tail”.
In the northern sky, late evening now: the Summer Triangle climbs in the east, the Big Dipper hangs handle-up in the northwest, and its curve leads to Arcturus and Spica in the west.
Next Wednesday: the Milky Way — a river, a road, a seam.
