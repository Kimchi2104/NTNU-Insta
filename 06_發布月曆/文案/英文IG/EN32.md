## 文案

In 1930, the whole sky was carved into 88 pieces. ✂️
It began with Ptolemy’s 48, listed nearly 1,900 years ago. But he never saw the far southern sky.
In the late 1500s, Dutch navigators charted it, and Plancius made 12 new constellations: a peacock, a toucan, a chameleon, a flying fish…
Hevelius’s 1690 star atlas filled northern and equatorial gaps — for the Lynx, he said, you need a lynx’s eyes. In the 1750s Lacaille, at the Cape of Good Hope, added 14: almost all instruments, plus one mountain, Table Mountain (Mensa).
In 1922 the IAU set the list of 88. Then Belgian astronomer Delporte drew the borders, published in 1930: straight lines, no gaps. Every point in the sky now belongs to exactly one constellation — and Hydra is 19 times the size of the Southern Cross.
