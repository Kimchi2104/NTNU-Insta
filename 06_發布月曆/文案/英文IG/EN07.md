## 文案

No sunrise for a month and a half — so they read the time in the stars. ✨
At Igloolik, 69° N in Arctic Canada, the sun stays down from late November to mid-January: Tauvikjuaq, the Great Darkness.
One elder recalled aiming a harpoon at the North Star, Nuutuittuq, “the one that never moves.” By morning the caribou (the Big Dipper) had shifted across the sky. The harpoon still pointed at the star.
Before dawn, children were sent out to check: is Aagjuuk (Altair and Tarazed) up? When it was, the day’s work began.
In many Inuit dialects, “tomorrow” is qauppat: “if it becomes day.”
