## 文案

1976: no compass, no map — 34 days to Tahiti. 🌌
The canoe was named Hōkūleʻa — Arcturus, “star of gladness.” Navigator Nainoa Thompson later split the sky into four star lines — not an ancient chart, but a modern memory aid. When Hōkūleʻa passes straight overhead, you are at Hawaiʻi’s latitude.
Winter’s line is Makaliʻi’s bailer: each November, when the Pleiades rise at sunset, Makahiki — the Hawaiian new year season — begins.
