## 文案

They join the stars — and the dark between. 🌌
For the Kamilaroi and Euahlayi peoples of northern New South Wales, the Coalsack beside the Southern Cross is the head of an emu, Gawarrgay. Its neck, body and legs are the dark lanes of the Milky Way, all the way to Scorpius and Sagittarius.
Its pose is a calendar: in April–May it runs; June–August it sits on the nest (time to collect eggs); in late August it stands upright as two eggs (stop — the chicks are about to hatch); in November it sits in the waterhole (the waterholes are full).
This month, after midnight, look south: from mid-northern latitudes the head never rises, but the body and legs do.
We share only what has been made public, mostly by Euahlayi Elder Ghillar Michael Anderson and the researchers he wrote with.
