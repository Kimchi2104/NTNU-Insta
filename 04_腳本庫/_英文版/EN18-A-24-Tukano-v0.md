## Caption (Instagram / Facebook / Threads)

In the Amazon, the floods are named after the stars. 🌧️
On the Tiquié River in northwest Brazil, the Tukano care less about when a constellation rises than when it sets. After sunset they look west: what is sinking now? A setting often brings a few days of rain and a flood — poero. When the armadillo sets, it’s pamõ poero, the armadillo’s flood.
February: a little fish and the shrimp chasing it. March–April: the jaguar, piece by piece. Late April: the Pleiades, and a new year. September to November: Aña, the venomous snake, with red Antares as its venom sac. December: the armadillo, also a sacred flute.
This week after dark, look low in the northwest: the west half of Cassiopeia’s W is the jaguar’s head.

## Cuts and changes (for the reviewer)

Dropped from the Chinese version (all still in the Chinese episode): the bridge from the Romania episode (01 鏡); the map of the Tiquié and the neighbouring peoples (02 鏡, C-A24-01; kept only "northwest Brazil, the Tiquié River" and the fish); Walmir Cardoso's 2005–06 visits and measuring the sky by hand (03 鏡, C-A24-02; he is credited in the sources); why the equator is good for this — every star rises and sets, straight down (06 鏡, C-A24-04); the fish rack (Hyades) and the adze handle (Orion), and the neighbours' myth of the snake-bitten leg (09–10 鏡); the egret and the dry months (11 鏡); the snake's head cut off in Corvus and "each part that sets brings a flood" (12 鏡); thunder and fish swimming upstream when the armadillo sets, and "Cardoso did not publish it, we draw only the public stars" (13 鏡; kept as a drawing rule, not on screen); siõka, the light that leads a constellation, and Jupiter in 2006 (15 鏡); meteors as star people on a date, and Cardoso's Tukano name (16 鏡, C-A24-06); the Taipei viewing card with clock times and altitudes (17 鏡, C-A24-08); the Pleiades–Hyades–Orion line with Jupiter and Mars (18 鏡); the Chinese Tupi teaser (19 鏡; replaced by next Wednesday's English episode).

Wording to check:
1. Cover "The Tukano saw — a whole year of floods." = 統一開頭「圖卡諾人看見——一整年的漲水」.
2. E02 = 02 鏡「巴西西北角……有一條河叫 Tiquié。河邊住著圖卡諾人和他們的鄰居，餐桌上最重要的肉，是河裡的魚」 (neighbours dropped).
3. E03/E04 = 04 鏡「他們最看重的，不是星座升起，而是它落下。太陽一下山，就往西邊看：哪個星座正要沉進地平線？」.
4. E05 headline keeps the hedge 「常常」 → "often": 05 鏡「一個星座或星座的一段落下，常常就跟著幾天雨，河水漲一次」.
5. E06 = 05 鏡「漲水直接用星座取名……pamõ poero——犰狳的漲水」.
6. E07 = 07 鏡「二月落下的，是一條小魚和追著牠的蝦」 ("both faint stars" dropped). Months throughout are the months of setting at dusk on the middle Tiquié (Cardoso table 3.01), as in the Chinese.
7. E08 headline "Mar–Apr: the jaguar, piece by piece" = 07 鏡「三、四月換美洲豹：鬍鬚、頭、身體、尾巴，一段一段沉下去」.
8. E09 "The Tukano here say the new year begins" = 08 鏡「這裡的圖卡諾人說：昴宿落下，新的一年就開始了」 (the "here" keeps the local scope).
9. E10 = 12 鏡「九月到十一月……毒蛇 Aña，從天蠍連到人馬……紅色的心宿二，是牠的毒囊」 ("the longest one" dropped).
10. E11 = 13 鏡「十二月，犰狳落下……犰狳也是神聖的笛子：這個星座在天上真正的樣子，只有男人才知道」. The drawing keeps to Cardoso's public stars (Altair trio + Delphinus), no flute shape.
11. E12: the ring's centre line "The sky reads almost like a river" = 14 鏡「天上寫的，幾乎就是一條河」.
12. E13 is the Northern-Hemisphere version of 17 鏡 (Taipei 19:13–20:30, northwest-by-north): around 10 March at mid-northern latitudes Cassiopeia stands low in the northwest after dark (higher than from Taipei, since it is circumpolar there). No clock times.
13. E14 = 17 鏡「這幾天在 Tiquié，牠正碰到地平線；照圖卡諾的日曆，這正是美洲豹的漲水」. The Chinese computes this for 5 March (whiskers 3.8°, Caph 5.5° at 19:14); Cardoso's table puts the head's setting in the first half of March, so it still holds for 10 March.
14. E15: EN19 (A-25 Tupi-Guarani) publishes Wednesday 3/17, so "Next Wednesday".
15. Diagrams C-A24-03 and C-A24-05: the English strings were already in `make_a24_diagrams.py`; `--lang en` now outputs just these two. Ring fixes: "fer-de-lance (Sco + Sgr)" → "snake (Sco + Sgr)" and "group of stars (Pleiades)" → "star group (Pleiades)" (both ran off the edge), the missing space in "The sky reads almost like a river", "★ New Year" moved off the blue arc, and the katakana dot in "Mhʉã・Dahsiʉ" → " · " (the English font has no glyph for it).
