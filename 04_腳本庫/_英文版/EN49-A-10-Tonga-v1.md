  Tonga’s old star names survive almost entirely in one sailing guide by high chief Tukuʻaho (Tonga’s premier, 1890–93), and some of them are sailing orders. From Tonga the Pole Star never rises, so the way north uses other stars: Kapakau ʻo Tafahi, like a hen with wings spread, brooding over the island of Tafahi; and ʻAo ʻo ʻUvea, a ring of small stars to be used only when it sits over ʻUvea like a headband. ## Cuts and changes (for the reviewer)

Dropped from the Chinese version (all still in the Chinese episode): the “last week, Hawaiʻi” bridge; the whole southern sky (03–07: the two fires Maʻafulele / Maʻafutoka and the Magellanic Clouds, Toloa the wild duck = the Southern Cross, the two men Ongo Tangata and the stone Maka, the south-pointing method, the Hawaiian “pointers” and Māori “anchor rope”); the joke about Stellarium’s note on the Pole Star (08, only the fact kept); Tuʻulalupe the pigeon roost (12); ʻAlotolu and Hina’s boat, Tuinga ika (13); Mataliki, the new year and the yam leap month (14); the Collocott-vs-Stellarium comparison and card C-A10-03 (15, kept only as “Sources differ / Stellarium guesses”); the Taipei sky details (16: “40° higher than in Tonga”, the Pleiades and Hyades in the east, the duck from Kenting in spring); the next-Friday Anuta teaser (replaced by next Wednesday’s English episode).

Wording to check:
1. E01 “sailing routes” = 統一開頭「一條條航線」.
2. E02 headline “about 170 islands” = 02「一百七十座左右的島」; “astronomy was part of navigation” = 「把天文當成航海的一部分」.
3. E03 “survives almost only in a sailing guide” = 「幾乎只留在一份航海指南裡」; “late-19th-century high chief Tukuʻaho” (v1; v0 “written … in the 1890s” wrongly dated the guide: the Chinese fact-check #4 says the writing year is unknown; Tukuʻaho was premier 1890–93). Collocott (1922) is dropped from the screen and kept in the description and the diagram source line.
4. E04 “Some star names are sailing orders” = 「有些東加星名，本身就是一句航海指令」.
5. E06 “more than 20° below the horizon” = 08「從東加本島看，北極星永遠在地平線下二十度以上」. The headline says “in Tonga”; the Chinese 對時 note gives Vavaʻu 18–19° and Niua 15–17°, so the body says “From Tonga’s main island” (v1; v0’s bare “From Tonga” was the error Chinese fact-check #2 fixed).
6. E07 “Mid-November, 22:00, on Vavaʻu island: a W sits about 10° above the northern sea” = 09「十一月中、晚上十點，站在北邊的 Vavaʻu 島往北看：海面上方十度上下，蹲著一個 W」.
7. E08 “like a hen, wings spread, brooding over Tafahi island” = 10「像一隻母雞，張開翅膀孵在 Tafahi 島上」.
8. E09 headline “Too far to see the island” = 「遠到看不見島，只看得見星」; 313 km in the diagram = 「三百多公里外」.
9. E10 “Which eight stars? Sources differ. Stellarium guesses Cassiopeia.” = 「是哪八顆星，各家說法不同，Stellarium 猜是仙后座」 (hedge kept).
10. E11 “circlet” = Collocott’s word (the Chinese 圓環; Stellarium says “cloud”); “Sail by it only when it sits over ʻUvea like a headband” = 「要等它像頭環一樣戴在 ʻUvea 的上空，才拿來駕船」.
11. E13 “highest in the northern sky on July evenings” = 「七月的晚上，北冕座正好升到北方最高」 (seen from Tongatapu, 7/1 21:39, 42°).
12. E14 sky tip rewritten for northern mid-latitudes, end of January: the Chinese says “8 p.m. tonight in Taipei (13 Nov): Cassiopeia high in the north”. By 31 Jan the W has swung to the northwest in the evening (the schedule note says the same: 仙后在西北). “Stellarium’s guess” keeps the hedge. Please check the direction wording.
13. The cover’s second line is a new hook (the schedule had “依中文 v2 再定”); it restates E04.
14. Page sequence is the authority for timing (total 89 s). The Sec column in Beats is not additive where a block spans pages.
