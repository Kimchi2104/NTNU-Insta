## YouTube

- **Title**: Inuit: telling time in the polar night
- **Description**:
  At Igloolik in Arctic Canada (69° N), the sun does not rise from late November to mid-January. With no sun and no clock, hunters read the time in the stars: an elder once aimed a harpoon at the North Star overnight; by morning the caribou (the Big Dipper) had swung round, but the harpoon still pointed at the star. Before dawn, Aagjuuk (Altair and Tarazed) tells the day's work to begin, and its first December dawn meant the solstice.
  World Sky Cultures: the same sky, seen by different peoples. From the NTNU Astronomy Club, Taiwan. New episode every Wednesday.
  Sources: John MacDonald, The Arctic Sky: Inuit Astronomy, Star Lore, and Legend (1998), Igloolik oral history; Stellarium sky culture (inuit).

## Cuts and changes (for the reviewer)

Dropped from the Chinese version (all still in the Chinese episode): the "last week in the desert" bridge; the polar-night diagram and the Taipei sun at 41° (a Taiwan comparison, diagram C-A14-01 not used); qausiut, "indicators of dawn"; the North Star being too high for steering; the wolves of Pelly Bay in the text (label only); the collarbones Quturjuuk and their tilting clock (label only; diagram C-A14-02 not used); the lamp stand and the seal-oil bag (labels only, on a transition page); "seen in autumn but ignored" and the first-sighting diagram (C-A14-03 not used); the Alaskan "two sunbeams" and the carrying poles of China, Anuta and Romania (labels only); the bear hunt, the lost mitten and Kingulliq; Sirius and Vega and the drifting ice; the return of the sun, the relit lamps and the half-smile; the star-name dictionary card; the Taipei "this weekend" guide; the Friday Chinese teaser (replaced by next Wednesday's English episode).

Wording to check:
1. Cover: "The Inuit saw — a polar-night clock" adapts 「同一片星空，因紐特人拿它——在極夜裡讀時間」 (they used it to read time) into the series' *One sky. The [people] saw — [X].* pattern. The cover's second line is the schedule's hook (*No sunrise for six weeks — so they read the time in the stars.*), not a translation. **The hook's "six weeks" is the schedule's wording**: the Chinese has 「一個半月」 (MacDonald: 29 Nov – 14 Jan, 46 days ≈ 6.6 weeks); the beats themselves say "late November … mid-January".
2. E02–E03 "From late November, the sun stays down. It comes back only in mid-January" = 「十一月底，太陽一沉下去就不再升起，要等一個半月、到一月中才回來」; "Arctic Canada" = 「進北極圈，到加拿大的 Igloolik」.
3. E04 "Hunters left a small hole in the igloo" = 「獵人會在冰屋上留個小洞」 (not "in the roof", as the Chinese notes).
4. E05 "An elder recalled aiming a harpoon at the North Star one night, propped by a snow wall" = 「有位長老回憶：他……晚上就把一支魚叉對準它，架在擋風的雪牆邊」 (his age at the time is not given, so not "as a boy").
5. E06 "Old hunters say: near midnight, it rears up on its hind legs" = 「老獵人說：『快到半夜，馴鹿會用後腳站起來』」; "clock hand" = 「時鐘的指針」.
6. E07 "the caribou had moved right round" = 「馴鹿整個換了位置」.
7. E09 "the key clock stars rise in the northeast" = 「天亮前最重要的報時星，在東北方的地平線上」.
8. E10 "Children went out before dawn to check" = 「孩子們天沒亮就被叫出門看」 ("were sent out" made the box four lines); "work began" = 「一天的工作就開始了」; "Aagjuuk has risen" for aagjuliqtuq = 「Aagjuuk 出來了」.
9. E11 keeps the hedge 「在老一輩眼裡」 = "For older Inuit"; "its first dawn in December meant the solstice" compresses 「Aagjuuk 第一次在清晨露臉，就是一年最短的那幾天」; "In 1990, an elder said so on Igloolik radio" = 「一九九〇年十二月十九號，一位老人家在 Igloolik 的社區廣播宣布：冬至到了——因為他看見了 Aagjuuk」.
10. E12 is the one rewritten line (Northern-Hemisphere mid-latitudes, not Taipei): the Chinese has Taipei 19 Dec, Altair 23° in the west at 18:30 and setting at 20:12. At 35–50° N in late December Altair and Tarazed are likewise in the west at dusk and set in the evening. Please check this is acceptable.
11. E13 "In many Inuit dialects" keeps 「在很多因紐特方言裡」; the Chinese line ends 「所以他們抬頭，讀星星」, dropped for time.
