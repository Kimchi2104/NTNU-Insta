## YouTube

- **Title**: Ancient Egypt: the star that woke the Nile
- **Description**:
  Every spring Sirius, Sopdet to the ancient Egyptians, vanished into the sunset glow for about 70 days. When it flashed back above the horizon before dawn, the Nile began to rise, and the Egyptian year originally began on that day. In the pyramid age that was a few days after the summer solstice; today, in Cairo, Sirius comes back around 5 August, over a month later. And since the Aswan High Dam was finished in 1970, the yearly flood stays in a reservoir.
  World Sky Cultures: the same sky, seen by different peoples. From the NTNU Astronomy Club, Taiwan. New episode every Wednesday.
  Sources: Stellarium sky culture “egyptian” (K. Berglund, after J. A. Belmonte); Censorinus, De Die Natali 21.10; rising dates computed with PyEphem.

## Cuts and changes (for the reviewer)

Dropped from the Chinese version (all still in the Chinese episode): the “last week on the Great Plains” bridge; the 365-day civil calendar and its three seasons (05), the 1,460-year cycle and Censorinus’s year 139 (06, kept only in the first comment); Sah becoming Osiris and Sopdet Isis (09); the 36 decans and the 12 hours of the night (10); the Bull’s Foreleg (Big Dipper), the mooring post and the hippopotamus (11); the Imperishable Stars and the pyramid’s north entrance (12); the Great Pyramid squared to north (13); the cord-stretching rite (14); the Dendera zodiac, Napoleon’s scholars, Champollion and the Cleopatra-era date (15–17); the full Moon, Canopus and the Foreleg in the sky tip (18); the Taipei card C-A18-10; the Chinese next-Friday teaser (replaced by next Wednesday’s English episode, EN12 Arabia).

Wording to check:
1. E01 “a star that woke the Nile” = Chinese 統一開頭「一顆叫醒尼羅河的星」.
2. E02 “Egypt gets almost no rain. Life depends on the Nile.” = 02「埃及幾乎不下雨，活命全靠尼羅河」. The summer rains upstream and “from June” are only implied by the diagram (“Summer: the river rises”).
3. E04 present tense “sinks … each spring” = 03「每年春天，它沉進夕陽的光裡」 (a sky fact, not a past custom).
4. E06 “originally” = 04「原本就從這一天開始」, kept because the civil calendar drifted (Chinese note ⑤); “Around then” = 「差不多就在這時候」.
5. E07 “a few days after the solstice, just in time for the flood” = 07「大約在夏至後幾天，正好趕上漲水」; the diagram gives Jun 28–29 vs solstice Jun 24 (Gregorian, proleptic). “We did the math” = 「我們算了一下」.
6. E09 “around 5 August” = 07「要等到八月五日前後」; E08 “over a month late” = 「晚了一個多月」 (38 days, our own computation, as in the Chinese).
7. E11 “In 1970 the Aswan High Dam was finished” = 08「一九七〇年亞斯文高壩完工」 (inaugurated Jan 1971 — the Chinese 考據備忘 note); “stays in a reservoir” = 「被攔進水庫」. The headline “The river no longer waits” = 題眼「等天狼星的河，已經不等了」.
8. E12 sky tip rewritten for northern mid-latitudes, late January: the Chinese says “10:30 tonight, Taipei, Sirius at its highest, 48°, Orion to its upper right”. The English drops the altitude (it depends on latitude) and says “around 10 p.m.” (Sirius transits about 22:20 local mean time on Jan 27). Please check this is acceptable as the “general Northern Hemisphere” wording.
9. E13 points to EN12 Ancient Arabia (Wed Feb 3), the Wednesday series, not to the Sunday re-run EN49 Tonga (Jan 31); same rule as EN48.
10. The cover’s second line is the schedule’s hook, not a translation of the Chinese cover.
11. Page sequence is the authority for timing (total 90 s). The Sec column in Beats is not additive where a block spans pages.
