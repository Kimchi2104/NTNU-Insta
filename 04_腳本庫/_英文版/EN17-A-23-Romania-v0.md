- **Description**:
  In 1896, Ion Otescu sent a star chart to village teachers all over Romania and asked them to find out from the oldest farmers what the stars were called. He found that farmers watched the stars mostly in summer, to tell time; the rest of the year, only the shepherds kept watching. Their winter sky is a farm: Capella is the she-goat with her three kids, Sirius sits on the sheepdog, the Pleiades are a hen with chicks, the Hyades a herd of boars, and Vega is the Shepherd with his sheep. The flock even has a well: the Great Square of Pegasus.

## Cuts and changes (for the reviewer)

Dropped from the Chinese version (all still in the Chinese episode): the bridge from the Belarus episode (01 鏡); Otescu as a mathematics educator (02 鏡; kept only his name); Capella as the Latin "little she-goat" with two kids, and the Romanian fairy tale of the goat and her three kids (05 鏡); China's "Wolf" for Sirius (06 鏡); Orion's four tools — the little plough, the great auger, the sickle and the Three Saints (08 鏡); the Big Cart and the sky's votive lamp (09 鏡); Romulus and Remus vs Belarus' Poland and Lithuania (10 鏡); the clock times for Vega rising over Taipei (11 鏡); the princess carrying water on a yoke (Altair) and the swapped Cowherd and Weaver card (12–13 鏡, C-A23-02); the well half set at 19:10 (14 鏡, C-A23-06); the horse (Leo) with Jupiter and Mars, and the cowherd (Boötes) (15 鏡); everyone's star and the ballad Miorița (16–17 鏡, C-A23-03); the star-name dictionary (C-A23-04); the Taipei viewing card (C-A23-05); the Chinese Amazon teaser (20 鏡; replaced by next Wednesday's English episode).

Wording to check:
1. Cover "Romanian shepherds saw — a farm in the stars." = 統一開頭「羅馬尼亞的牧羊人看見——一座農場」.
2. E02 "sent a star chart to village teachers all over Romania" = 02 鏡「把星圖寄給全國的鄉村老師」 (1896 = Otescu's preface; the chart was drawn for Bucharest, as the English diagram says).
3. E03/E04: the headline "Ask the oldest farmers" + "The question: what are the stars called?" = 02 鏡「請他們去問村裡最老的農民：天上的星星叫什麼？」.
4. E06 "In summer, farmers told time by them. Otherwise, only shepherds kept watching." = 03 鏡「農民看星星，多半是在夏天農忙，拿星星報時；一年其他時候，還一直盯著星星的，只有牧羊人」. The diagram shows the months (Jun–Sep) and keeps the line "so farmers called them ‘the shepherds’ stars’", which is on the Chinese diagram.
5. E07 "High in the north, Capella is Capra" = 04 鏡「北邊高空的五車二」. Holds for northern mid-latitudes too (early March evenings Capella is near the zenith, slightly north-west).
6. E08 "sits on Dulăul, the big dog that guards the sheep" = 06 鏡「在看羊的大狗 Dulăul 身上」; "Procyon is a little dog" = 「南河三，是一隻小狗」 (the Chinese "左上方" dropped).
7. E10 keeps the hedge of 07 鏡「也有人叫它『鬧鐘』」 → "some say". "a herd of boars" = 「一群公豬」.
8. E11/E12: 11 鏡 says, for Taipei, that the Shepherd rises only after midnight. For northern mid-latitudes in early March Vega rises in the northeast roughly between 21:00 (50° N) and midnight (30° N), so the English says "only late at night, in the northeast" with no clock time.
9. E13 "So the sky has a well, too" = 14 鏡「羊要喝水，天上也有井」; the well is setting in the west in the evening (14 鏡), not said in the English.
10. E14 is the Northern-Hemisphere version of 19 鏡 (Taipei 20:00 / 05:00): in early March at mid-northern latitudes, after dark Sirius is in the south and Capella near the zenith; before dawn (about 05:00) Vega is 50–60° up in the east-northeast. No clock times, no planets (Jupiter and Mars are in Leo, which the English does not name).
11. E15: EN18 (A-24 Tukano) publishes Wednesday 3/10, so "Next Wednesday".
12. Diagram C-A23-01 (English): "1896: a star chart for the whole country"; "to village teachers / ask the oldest farmers"; "39 useful replies / 18 more from other counties"; year layer "When did farmers watch the stars?" (the old draft English said "peasants"; changed to "farmers" to match the on-screen text).
